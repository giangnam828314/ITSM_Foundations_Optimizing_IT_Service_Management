# ITIL 4 Foundation — Topic 2: Key Concepts of ITIL

## 1. Introduction

Along with the four dimensions of service management, the **Service Value System (SVS)** is a central element of the ITIL framework. This topic introduces the major components of the SVS that will be examined in more detail later.

---

# 2. Opportunity and Demand

**Opportunity** and **demand** trigger the Service Value System.

### Opportunity

An **opportunity** is an option or possibility to add value for stakeholders or otherwise improve the organization. There may not yet be demand for the opportunity.

Examples in the context of the lesson include opportunities for new or changed services and opportunities for improvement.

### Demand

**Demand** is input to the SVS based on opportunities and needs from internal and external stakeholders. In other words, demand is the need or desire for products and services from internal and external customers.

### Tư duy

```text
Opportunity + Demand
          ↓
     Service Value System
          ↓
        VALUE
```

# 3. Guiding Principles

## What is a Guiding Principle?

Guiding principles are **recommendations that can guide an organization in all circumstances**, regardless of changes in its goals, strategies, type of work, or management structure.

They can be used when planning and implementing initiatives to improve IT services. They are not exclusive to ITSM and can be applied broadly across industries and situations.

A framework or methodology does not create value simply by existing; its value comes from being put into practice.

The lesson notes that some principles from ITIL Practitioner were combined or modified in ITIL 4.

---

# 4. The Seven Guiding Principles

## 1. Focus on Value

Everything the organization does should map, directly or indirectly, to value for stakeholders. Follow the customers' lead on what they value and work toward increasing service value.

## 2. Start Where You Are

Do not start from scratch without considering what already exists. Leverage existing services, processes and people. Avoid recreating the wheel unnecessarily.

## 3. Progress Iteratively with Feedback

Do not attempt to do everything at once. Break projects, changes or efforts into manageable chunks so learning, feedback and testing can be used throughout the work.

## 4. Collaborate and Promote Visibility

Working across organizational boundaries improves buy-in, relevance to objectives and the likelihood of long-term success. Make improvement initiatives and their reasoning visible.

## 5. Think and Work Holistically

No service or service component stands alone. Consider the entire project and all related components because they are interrelated and interdependent.

## 6. Keep It Simple and Practical

If a process, service, action or metric provides no value or produces no useful outcome, eliminate it.

## 7. Optimize and Automate

Human intervention should happen only where it really contributes value.

### Mẹo nhớ

> **Value → Start → Iterate → Collaborate → Holistic → Simple → Optimize**

The guiding principles complement other frameworks and standards such as **Lean Six Sigma, Agile and DevOps**.

# 5. Governance

Governance is the **means by which an organization is directed and controlled**.

It can apply to an entire organization or to units/products within it.

The guiding principles and continual improvement apply across the entire SVS, including governance.

A governing body can:

- adopt the ITIL guiding principles;
- adapt them;
- define its own specific principles;
- communicate those principles across the organization.

Governance provides visibility into the outcomes of continual improvement and measurement of value for the organization and its stakeholders.

### Governance and management

The Service Value Chain and practices operate according to direction from the governing body. The governing body maintains oversight of the SVS, directly or through delegated authority.

Governance and management at all levels maintain alignment through shared principles and objectives and are continually improved to meet stakeholder expectations.

# 6. Service Value Chain (SVC)

The **Service Value Chain (SVC)** is composed of **six activities** and provides the operating model for service management.

It is triggered by demand and results in products and services that provide value.

The activities are used in different combinations depending on what is required by the customer.

> In ITIL 4, the earlier **Service Lifecycle** is replaced by the combination of the **SVS, Service Value Chain and Four Dimensions of Service Management**.

## Six Service Value Chain Activities

| Activity | Description |
|---|---|
| **Plan** | Relevant planning at strategic, architectural, program/project and other levels. |
| **Improve** | Execution of improvement activities and provision of improvement information. |
| **Engage** | Interactions with users, customers, suppliers and other stakeholders. |
| **Design and Transition** | Creation of new/changed solution designs and management/implementation of change. |
| **Obtain and Build** | Acquisition or building of components, from third parties or in-house. |
| **Deliver and Support** | Hosting and delivery of solutions and support for user needs related to services. |

### ⭐ Phải thuộc

> **Plan – Improve – Engage – Design & Transition – Obtain & Build – Deliver & Support**

# 7. ITIL Practices

An ITIL **practice** is:

> A set of organizational resources designed for performing work or accomplishing an objective.

In ITIL 4, practices are no longer linked to a single value chain activity as they were in ITIL 3.

There are **34 practices**, organized into three categories:

1. **General Management Practices**
2. **Service Management Practices**
3. **Technical Management Practices**

### Ten examinable Service Management Practices

1. **Change Enablement** — ensuring risks are properly assessed, changes are authorized and the change schedule is managed to maximize successful IT changes.
2. **Incident Management** — minimizing negative impact by restoring normal service operation as quickly as possible.
3. **IT Asset Management** — planning and managing the full lifecycle of IT assets.
4. **Monitoring and Event Management** — systematically observing services/service components and recording/reporting selected state changes as events.
5. **Problem Management** — reducing likelihood and impact of incidents by identifying actual/potential causes and managing workarounds and known errors.
6. **Release Management** — making new and changed services/features available for use.
7. **Service Configuration Management** — ensuring accurate and reliable configuration information is available when and where needed.
8. **Service Desk** — capturing demand for incident resolution and service requests.
9. **Service Level Management** — setting clear business-based targets for service performance so delivery can be assessed, monitored and managed.
10. **Service Request Management** — handling predefined, user-initiated service requests effectively and in a user-friendly manner.

# 11. Continual Improvement

The **Continual Improvement model applies to the SVS in its entirety**.

It includes:

- products;
- services;
- service components;
- relationships.

ITIL addresses continual improvement in three ways:

### 1. ITIL Continual Improvement model

Provides a structured approach for implementing improvements.

### 2. Improve activity in the Service Value Chain

Embeds continual improvement into the value chain.

### 3. Continual Improvement practice

Supports day-to-day improvement efforts.

### ⭐ Exam memory

> **Continual Improvement Model + Improve Activity + Continual Improvement Practice**

# 12. Activity 2-2 — Discussing the Service Value System

## Question 1
**When considering the two major components of the SVS, is one of them more important or critical to service management?**

The question is designed to test understanding of the relationship between SVS components rather than treating one isolated component as sufficient.

## Question 2
**In the SVS, what is the difference between a practice and a principle?**

### Answer

> A **practice** is a set of organizational resources designed for performing work or accomplishing an objective, while a **principle** provides guidance that should be applied to all circumstances.

## Question 3
**Which guiding principle has the major point of service management incorporated into its name?**

### Answer

> **Focus on Value**

## Question 4
**What are the six activities in the Service Value Chain?**

### Answer

> **Plan, Engage, Improve, Obtain/Build, Design and Transition, and Deliver and Support.**

## Question 5
**Why is governance a fundamental part of the SVS?**

### Answer

> **All aspects of the SVS are subject to the overall governance of the organization.**

## Question 6
**What are the three ways that continual improvement is addressed in ITIL and the SVS?**

### Answer

1. **The Continual Improvement model**
2. **The Improve activity in the Service Value Chain**
3. **The Continual Improvement practice**

# 14. Liên hệ với ITSM thực tế

Topic này chuyển từ khái niệm SVS sang cách một ITSM platform thực sự vận hành.

```text
OPPORTUNITY / DEMAND
          ↓
       GOVERNANCE
          ↓
   GUIDING PRINCIPLES
          ↓
  SERVICE VALUE CHAIN
          ↓
 ┌────────┼─────────┐
 ↓        ↓         ↓
PLAN    ENGAGE    IMPROVE
 ↓        ↓         ↓
DESIGN & TRANSITION
          ↓
   OBTAIN & BUILD
          ↓
 DELIVER & SUPPORT
          ↓
        VALUE
          ↑
      PRACTICES
          ↑
 CONTINUAL IMPROVEMENT
```

Điểm quan trọng cho kiến trúc ITSM/MSP là **practice không bị khóa vào một activity duy nhất của Service Value Chain**. Một practice có thể hỗ trợ nhiều activities tùy context.

Ví dụ:

- Incident Management → hỗ trợ Deliver and Support nhưng có thể liên quan đến Improve.
- Change Enablement → liên quan Design and Transition, Obtain and Build, Deliver and Support tùy loại change.
- Service Level Management → liên quan Engage và Improve, đồng thời cung cấp input cho các hoạt động khác.
- Monitoring and Event Management → hỗ trợ Deliver and Support và tạo input cho Incident/Problem/Continual Improvement.

Đây là tư duy quan trọng khi thiết kế một ITSM platform: **không xây phần mềm theo kiểu mỗi module = một value chain activity**.
