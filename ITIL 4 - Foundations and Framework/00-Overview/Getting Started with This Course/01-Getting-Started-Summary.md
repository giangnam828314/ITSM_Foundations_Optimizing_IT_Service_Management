# Getting Started with This Course — ITIL 4 Foundation

## 1. Course purpose

ITIL® is best-practice guidance for IT service management (ITSM). The course introduces the foundational knowledge and skills needed to adopt and adapt ITSM best practices in an organization, with a focus on creating value for customers and stakeholders.

## 2. Who should take the course?

The course is intended for:
- IT staff and IT management who need a foundation in IT service management.
- Customers/business stakeholders who work closely with IT.
- Learners preparing for the ITIL 4 Foundation certification exam.

## 3. What the course covers

The course is organized around the seven ITIL 4 Foundation learning outcomes:

1. **Key concepts of service management**
2. **ITIL guiding principles**
3. **Four dimensions of service management**
4. **ITIL Service Value System (SVS)**
5. **Service Value Chain and its activities**
6. **Purpose and key terms of 15 ITIL practices**
7. **Detailed understanding of 7 ITIL practices**

### Easy distinctions

| Concept | Remember it as |
|---|---|
| **Utility** | What the service does — fit for purpose |
| **Warranty** | How the service performs — fit for use |
| **Output** | Deliverable produced by an activity |
| **Outcome** | Result enabled for a stakeholder |
| **Incident** | Unplanned interruption/reduction in service quality |
| **Problem** | Cause or potential cause of one or more incidents |
| **Known error** | A problem that has been analyzed but not resolved |

## 5. Seven guiding principles

1. Focus on value
2. Start where you are
3. Progress iteratively with feedback
4. Collaborate and promote visibility
5. Think and work holistically
6. Keep it simple and practical
7. Optimize and automate

These are recommendations that guide an organization in all circumstances; they are not mandatory prescriptive processes.

## 6. Four dimensions of service management

1. **Organizations and people** — roles, responsibilities, structure, culture, skills.
2. **Information and technology** — information, knowledge, systems, technology and supporting capabilities.
3. **Partners and suppliers** — relationships, contracts and dependencies with external parties.
4. **Value streams and processes** — activities, workflows, controls and procedures used to create value.

## 7. Service Value System (SVS)

The SVS represents how the components and activities of an organization work together to facilitate value creation. The main elements to connect mentally are:

**Guiding Principles + Governance + Service Value Chain + Practices + Continual Improvement → Value Co-Creation**

## 8. Service Value Chain

The six value chain activities are:

- **Plan** — shared understanding of vision, current status and improvement direction.
- **Improve** — continual improvement of products, services and practices.
- **Engage** — understand stakeholder needs, maintain transparency and relationships.
- **Design & Transition** — ensure services/products meet expectations for quality, cost and time to market.
- **Obtain/Build** — ensure required service components are available and meet specifications.
- **Deliver & Support** — deliver and support services according to agreed specifications and stakeholder expectations.

The activities are interconnected. They are not a single mandatory linear workflow; different combinations can form different value streams.

## 10. Seven practices to understand in detail

These are especially important because Learning Outcome 7 carries **17 of the 40 exam marks**:

- Continual improvement
- Change enablement
- Incident management
- Problem management
- Service request management
- Service desk
- Service level management

### High-priority practice distinctions

**Incident Management** → restore normal service operation as quickly as possible.

**Problem Management** → reduce likelihood and impact of incidents by identifying causes and managing workarounds/known errors.

**Change Enablement** → maximize successful changes through risk assessment, authorization and change scheduling.

**Service Request Management** → handle predefined, user-initiated service requests; standard changes are often fulfilled through service requests.

**Service Desk** → entry point and single point of contact for users; captures demand for incident resolution and service requests.

**Service Level Management** → set clear business-based service targets and assess, monitor and manage delivery against them.

**Continual Improvement** → continually align practices and services with changing business needs; improvement decisions should be based on accurate, carefully analyzed data.

## 11. Exam format

The Candidate Syllabus specifies:

- **40 multiple-choice questions**
- **60 minutes**
- **1 mark per question**
- **26 correct answers (65%) to pass**
- **No negative marking**
- **Closed book**
- Bloom's Levels **1 and 2**: recall and understanding

Question styles include classic, negative, missing-word and list questions.

## 12. What the sample papers teach us

The two official sample papers repeatedly test:

- precise definitions;
- differences between similar practices/terms;
- correct purpose of practices;
- selection of the right guiding principle for a situation;
- value chain activity purpose and interaction;
- service relationships;
- incident/problem/change distinctions;
- application of ITIL concepts to practical scenarios.

The rationale documents are important because they explain not only why an answer is correct, but why the other options are incorrect.

## 13. Important exam traps

- **Utility ≠ Warranty**: what the service does vs. how it performs.
- **Incident ≠ Problem**: restore service vs. address cause/potential cause.
- **Problem ≠ Known Error**: known error is a problem already analyzed but not resolved.
- **Change ≠ Release ≠ Deployment**: change controls an addition/modification/removal; release makes new/changed services or features available; deployment moves components into an environment.
- **Service Desk ≠ Incident Management**: Service Desk is the contact point; Incident Management restores normal service.
- **Value Chain ≠ one fixed workflow**: activities are interconnected and can be combined differently.
- **Optimize and Automate**: understand the context/current state and optimize before automating.
- **Guiding principles are recommendations**, not mandatory prescriptive processes.
